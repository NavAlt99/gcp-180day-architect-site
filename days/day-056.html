Cloud VPN (Classic vs HA VPN, 99.99% SLA topology)

Cloud VPN securely extends on-premises networks to Google Cloud VPCs over encrypted IPsec tunnels across the public internet. High Availability (HA) VPN delivers a contractually guaranteed 99.99% service level agreement (SLA) by provisioning two distinct public IPv4 gateway interfaces (interface 0 and interface 1) anchored to physically isolated Google hardware clusters and power domains. HA VPN mandates dynamic Border Gateway Protocol (eBGP) routing managed by Google Cloud Router, supporting active/active dual-tunnel Equal-Cost Multi-Path (ECMP) forwarding or deterministic active/passive failover using Multi-Exit Discriminator (MED) path metrics. In contrast, legacy Classic VPN provides only a single gateway interface with static routing, single tunnel failure domains, and a lower 99.9% availability SLA, making it entirely unsuitable for mission-critical enterprise workloads.

During scheduled hypervisor maintenance on a single-interface Classic VPN gateway, the sole IPsec tunnel drops packet forwarding for 18 minutes while awaiting host re-anchoring. The disruption halts warehouse inventory synchronization across 42 distribution centers, stranding $1.8M in pending truck dispatches until routes manually restabilize.

Cloud Interconnect (Dedicated vs Partner, MACsec, 10G/100G circuits)

Cloud Interconnect establishes enterprise-grade, high-bandwidth private physical connectivity directly into Google's global fiber backbone without traversing the public internet. Dedicated Interconnect provides 10 Gbps or 100 Gbps single-mode optical fiber cross-connects terminating directly on Google edge routing equipment located within certified Google colocation facilities (Meet-Me-Rooms), supporting optional line-rate MACsec (IEEE 802.1AE) Layer 2 hardware encryption. For enterprises not co-located in a Google facility, Partner Interconnect connects through certified service providers (such as Equinix, Megaport, or Console Connect) with flexible sub-interface VLAN attachments ranging from 50 Mbps up to 50 Gbps, offering rapid provisioning via pairing keys and a choice between Layer 2 and Layer 3 partner handoffs.

A central aggregation switch failure at a regional Partner Interconnect colocation facility terminates both virtual circuits carrying internal ERP traffic between on-premises headquarters and GCP. Core financial ledger processing halts for 9 hours during quarter-end reconciliation, triggering contractual non-compliance penalties exceeding $420,000.

Cross-Cloud Interconnect (AWS/Azure direct multi-cloud links)

Cross-Cloud Interconnect establishes dedicated, high-speed, SLA-backed physical cross-connects directly between Google Cloud and secondary cloud service providers, including Amazon Web Services (AWS Direct Connect) and Microsoft Azure (ExpressRoute). It completely eliminates the architectural overhead, physical equipment colocation leasing, and third-party telecom carrier contracts historically required to bridge multi-cloud infrastructure. Provisioned over 10 Gbps or 100 Gbps dedicated interfaces, Cross-Cloud Interconnect provides deterministic sub-3 millisecond latency, reduced cross-cloud data egress pricing, and zero exposure to public internet routing anomalies or MTU fragmentation.

Network Connectivity Center (NCC) router spoke integration

Network Connectivity Center (NCC) delivers a centralized hub-and-spoke transit management architecture that unifies hybrid enterprise networks, multi-region Virtual Private Clouds, and third-party virtual routing appliances under a single control plane. By attaching Cloud Routers and Router Appliance virtual machines (such as Palo Alto Networks VM-Series, Fortinet FortiGate, or Cisco Catalyst 8000V) as hybrid spokes to an NCC Hub, enterprises achieve dynamic spoke-to-spoke route redistribution and wide-area network (WAN) aggregation across distributed branch offices. NCC automatically aggregates BGP route announcements, dynamically resolving transitive routing without requiring manual point-to-point IPsec mesh overlays.

Enterprise branch offices experience unpredictable routing blackholes and split-brain path selection due to a fragmented full mesh of static VPN tunnels and uncoordinated Cloud Router BGP advertisements. Regional branch tellers lose database connectivity during peak retail banking hours, idling 14 branches and generating customer SLA breach reports.

99.9% vs 99.99% topologies and failure domain isolation

Achieving Google Cloud's formal availability SLAs requires rigorous physical and topological domain isolation; as the architectural adage states, "a drawing does not prove an SLA." The 99.9% SLA topology delivers high availability within a single metropolitan area by terminating redundant circuits across two separate Edge Availability Domains (EAD 1 and EAD 2), protecting against router, power, and optic failures while remaining vulnerable to municipal metro disasters. The mission-critical 99.99% SLA topology mandates geographic diversity across two distinct metropolitan areas (e.g., Ashburn and Chicago) connecting to two separate Google Cloud regions, requiring four VLAN attachments, dual Cloud Routers, Global Dynamic Routing, and deterministic BGP route priority tuning.

A municipal water main burst floods an Ashburn colocation facility, severing all fiber conduits entering the building and simultaneously taking down both Dedicated Interconnect circuits of a supposedly "resilient" single-metro deployment. The regional transit blackout suspends hospital patient scheduling systems for 11 hours and incurs a 25% enterprise insurance liability deduction.

Direct Peering vs Carrier Peering

Google Peering services establish direct BGP route exchange with Google's public Autonomous System (ASN 15169) to optimize performance for Google Workspace, YouTube, and public Google Cloud APIs without traversing intermediate transit internet service providers. Peering must never be confused with Cloud Interconnect: peering strictly exchanges public IP prefixes and cannot route traffic to private RFC 1918 VPC subnets or Compute Engine instances. Direct Peering connects enterprises that operate their own registered public ASN and maintain a physical presence at Google-supported Internet Exchange Points (IXPs) over minimum 10G circuits, whereas Carrier Peering enables organizations without a public ASN to access Google's public edge through certified telecommunications partners.

Engineers configure a Direct Peering session expecting on-premises core databases to replicate directly to private VPC instances, only to discover that RFC 1918 subnets are completely unreachable across the peering BGP session. The mistaken routing design delays a multi-million-dollar database migration by six weeks while emergency Dedicated Interconnect circuits are expedited.

Cloud VPN (Classic vs HA VPN, 99.99% SLA topology)

Google Cloud VPN securely connects an on-premises network or secondary cloud environment to a Google Cloud Virtual Private Cloud (VPC) through encrypted IPsec gateways operating over public internet infrastructure.

HA VPN Architecture and the 99.99% Availability SLA

High Availability (HA) VPN guarantees a contractually backed 99.99% service availability SLA when deployed according to Google's validated dual-tunnel topology:

  • Dual Independent Gateway Interfaces: A single HA VPN gateway resource automatically provisions two separate public IPv4 IP addresses (interface 0 and interface 1) anchored to physically distinct Google server clusters, edge routers, and electrical power domains within the chosen region.
  • Dynamic BGP Routing via Cloud Router: HA VPN mandates Cloud Router and dynamic exterior BGP (eBGP). Static routing is strictly unsupported. Cloud Router exchanges dynamic prefixes using standard BGP keepalive and hold timers (typically 20 seconds hold time on Cloud Router). If an IPsec tunnel or underlying network path experiences packet drop, Cloud Router detects peer withdrawal and reroutes egress packets to the surviving tunnel in under 2 seconds without terminating active TCP socket sessions.
  • Active/Active vs. Active/Passive Topologies:
    • Active/Active with ECMP: Both tunnels advertise identical BGP Multi-Exit Discriminator (MED) values (e.g., MED 100). Google Cloud routes outbound traffic across both tunnels simultaneously using 5-tuple flow hashing (source IP, destination IP, protocol, source port, destination port), effectively doubling aggregate tunnel throughput up to 6 Gbps.
    • Active/Passive with Asymmetric MED: The primary tunnel advertises MED 100 while the standby tunnel advertises MED 200. Cloud Router strictly forwards traffic over the primary tunnel, switching to the backup path only when BGP peering on the primary tunnel times out.
  • Mandatory 99.99% SLA Requirements: To legally qualify for Google's 99.99% SLA:
    1. The on-premises environment must deploy either two separate physical VPN gateways, OR a single peer gateway equipped with two distinct public IP interfaces.
    2. Two separate IPsec tunnels must be configured from GCP HA VPN: Tunnel 0 originating from interface 0 and Tunnel 1 originating from interface 1, terminating on separate peer IP interfaces.
    3. Independent IKE security associations (IKEv2 recommended) and distinct eBGP sessions (RFC 1918 or RFC 6598 link-local /30 or /31 peering addresses in 169.254.0.0/16) must run over each tunnel.

Why Classic VPN is Deprecated for Production

Classic VPN features a single gateway interface representing a single point of failure (SPOF). During Google Cloud routine host hypervisor maintenance or optical transceiver re-seating, the single public IP drops packet forwarding, causing an unavoidable 10 to 20 minute outage. Furthermore, Classic VPN only supports an inferior 99.9% availability SLA, relies heavily on static routes that require manual operational intervention during link failures, and does not support IPv6 dual-stack traffic or modern BGP communities. Consequently, Classic VPN is obsolete and prohibited in production enterprise landing zones.

Apply it

Input: Brightloaf connects a critical bakery factory router to GCP over HA VPN. During a fiber maintenance cut on Interface 0's path, what ensures traffic survival? Expected: Cloud Router detects BGP keepalive timeout on Interface 0, withdraws the route, and immediately directs outbound packets across Interface 1 with zero manual intervention.

Cloud Interconnect provides physical, enterprise-grade, high-bandwidth private network connections between an enterprise on-premises data center and Google's global private network backbone, bypassing the public internet entirely.

Procurement Workflow and the LOA-CFA Process

Ordering Dedicated Interconnect requires a structured physical deployment sequence:

  1. Order Submission: Request 10G or 100G ports in a specific colocation facility (e.g., Equinix Ashburn DC2) and specify Edge Availability Domain allocation.
  2. LOA-CFA Issuance: Google provisions the physical port on its edge router (PFE) and generates a Letter of Authorization and Connecting Facility Assignment (LOA-CFA) PDF.
  3. Cross-Connect Order: The customer delivers the LOA-CFA to the colocation facility management to run single-mode fiber patch cables between the customer cage and Google's Meet-Me-Room (MMR) patch panel.
  4. Light Attenuation and Optical Testing: Google executes automated optical transceiver light tests. Once attenuation falls within acceptable decibel loss thresholds, the port state moves to PROVISIONED.

VLAN Attachments (InterconnectAttachments)

A physical Interconnect circuit cannot transmit VPC traffic until an InterconnectAttachment (VLAN attachment) is allocated. Each attachment represents a dedicated 802.1Q VLAN tag (e.g., VLAN 1024), an associated Cloud Router, and a point-to-point BGP peering subnet in 169.254.0.0/16. Multiple VLAN attachments can be created on a single 100G physical circuit to connect multiple distinct VPCs or different projects within an organization.

Cross-Cloud Interconnect (AWS/Azure direct multi-cloud links)

Modern enterprise architectures frequently distribute transactional microservices, analytical data lakes, and disaster recovery environments across multiple public cloud providers. Traditionally, connecting Google Cloud to AWS Direct Connect or Microsoft Azure ExpressRoute required leasing expensive third-party colocation cage space, procuring physical routers, and establishing complex multi-party maintenance contracts in intermediate transit centers.

Direct Multi-Cloud Link Architecture

Google Cloud Cross-Cloud Interconnect establishes direct, managed physical cross-connects between Google's edge routers and AWS Direct Connect, Azure ExpressRoute, or Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) FastConnect in shared colocation facilities without requiring intermediate customer-managed physical hardware:

  • Turnkey Provisioning: Google manages the physical fiber cross-connect directly with AWS or Azure. The enterprise simply orders the port through Google Cloud, receives the partner circuit configuration details, and maps the connection in the secondary cloud's management console.
  • High Bandwidth and Line-Rate Predictability: Available in 10 Gbps and 100 Gbps port capacities, Cross-Cloud Interconnect delivers consistent, sub-3 millisecond round-trip latency, eliminating the unpredictable packet jitter and public internet congestion typical of unmanaged multi-cloud IPsec tunnels.
  • Discounted Inter-Cloud Egress: Network egress traffic exiting Google Cloud across Cross-Cloud Interconnect qualifies for discounted interconnect data transfer rates, substantially reducing operational spend compared to standard internet egress fees.
  • SLA Guarantees: Cross-Cloud Interconnect supports Google's 99.9% and 99.99% availability SLAs when redundant physical connections are provisioned across separate facilities and cloud regions.

Network Connectivity Center (NCC) router spoke integration

Network Connectivity Center (NCC) serves as an active, centralized transit routing fabric that unifies on-premises networks, multi-region Virtual Private Clouds, and third-party software-defined WAN (SD-WAN) appliances under an automated control plane.

Router Appliance Spoke Integration

Enterprise organizations frequently rely on virtualized third-party network appliances—such as Next-Generation Firewalls (NGFW) or SD-WAN edge routers from Palo Alto Networks (VM-Series), Fortinet (FortiGate), or Cisco (Catalyst 8000V)—to inspect and secure hybrid traffic. Prior to NCC, integrating these appliances required complex, error-prone static routing hacks or full-mesh point-to-point IPsec overlays.

With NCC Router Appliance Spokes:

  • BGP Peering with Cloud Router: Router Appliance VM instances run inside customer VPC subnets with multiple network interfaces (NICs). The VM establishes an internal eBGP peering session directly with Google Cloud Router over its primary interface.
  • Dynamic Route Exchange: Cloud Router advertises VPC subnet CIDRs to the Router Appliance VM, while the appliance VM advertises on-premises branch routes into Cloud Router.
  • Automated Spoke-to-Spoke Transit: NCC ingests routes from attached hybrid spokes (Dedicated Interconnect, Partner Interconnect, HA VPN, and Router Appliance instances) and automatically redistributes them to other VPC and hybrid spokes. Branch offices can communicate directly with on-premises data centers and other remote branches across Google's planetary backbone without requiring hairpin routing through an on-premises core switch.
Apply it

Input: An enterprise has 4 disparate Shared VPC networks and 45 on-premises retail branch offices connected via Fortinet SD-WAN appliances. Workloads in all VPCs must dynamically route to on-prem data centers without managing static route tables. Expected: Create an NCC Hub, add the 4 VPCs as VPC Spokes, and attach the Fortinet SD-WAN VMs as Router Appliance Spokes with Cloud Router eBGP peering.

99.9% vs 99.99% topologies and failure domain isolation

Understanding Google Cloud's contractual availability SLAs is an essential architecture competency. A critical engineering principle governs all hybrid landing zone designs: "A drawing does not prove an SLA." Simply drawing four redundant lines on an architectural diagram does not qualify an enterprise for Google's 99.99% availability SLA or financial SLA credit compensation unless every physical, geographic, and routing prerequisite is satisfied.

The 99.9% SLA Topology (Single Metro)

  • Geographic Scope: Two physical connections deployed within a single metropolitan area (e.g., Ashburn).
  • Edge Availability Domains: Connections must terminate in two separate Edge Availability Domains: EAD 1 (zone 1) and EAD 2 (zone 2). Google guarantees that separate EADs within a single metro possess independent edge routing chassis, distinct building power feeds, and physically separated optical distribution frames (ODFs).
  • Failure Domains Mitigated: Survives failure of a single edge router, optical transceiver blowout, port failure, or single rack power failure.
  • Vulnerability: DOES NOT SURVIVE catastrophic metropolitan events, such as a municipal contractor backhoe severing all fiber conduits entering the facility, a regional colocation power grid collapse, or localized severe flooding. Contractually permits up to 8.76 hours of downtime per year.

The 99.99% SLA Topology (Dual Metro / Dual Region)

  • Geographic Scope: Requires a minimum of four physical connections deployed across TWO DISTINCT METROPOLITAN AREAS (e.g., Metro A: Ashburn, VA; Metro B: Chicago, IL).
  • Edge Availability Domains: Within each metro, the two circuits must terminate in separate Edge Availability Domains (EAD 1 and EAD 2).
  • Dual Cloud Routers across Dual Regions: VLAN attachments in Metro A connect to Cloud Router 1 in Region A (e.g., us-east4); VLAN attachments in Metro B connect to Cloud Router 2 in Region B (e.g., us-central1).
  • Global Dynamic Routing: The VPC dynamic routing mode must be set to GLOBAL, ensuring that if Metro A is completely severed, Cloud Router 2 in Region B dynamically advertises and forwards traffic to subnets in all regions worldwide.
  • BGP MED Route Priority Design: To prevent erratic packet spraying and asymmetric routing, the enterprise configures custom BGP route priorities:
    • Metro A (Primary): Advertised with lower MED (e.g., MED 100).
    • Metro B (Secondary Standby): Advertised with higher MED (e.g., MED 200).
  • Surviving Capacity Formula: The enterprise must ensure that each metropolitan deployment independently possesses sufficient bandwidth to absorb 100% of peak enterprise production traffic if the alternate metro collapses: Surviving Capacity = min(Metro_A_Capacity, Metro_B_Capacity) >= Peak Production Traffic

Direct Peering vs Carrier Peering

Enterprise network engineers frequently confuse Google Peering with Google Cloud Interconnect. While both involve physical connectivity to Google, their architectural objectives, routing boundaries, and reachable IP spaces are fundamentally distinct.

Peering vs. Interconnect: The Architectural Demarcation

  • Cloud Interconnect connects to Private VPC Networks: Interconnect is designed exclusively for extending internal corporate RFC 1918, RFC 6598, and custom private subnets to Google Cloud Virtual Private Clouds (Compute Engine VMs, GKE pods, internal load balancers).
  • Google Peering connects to Google Public Services: Peering exchanges public BGP routes with Google's public Autonomous System (ASN 15169). It provides direct, low-latency network access to Google Workspace (Gmail, Drive, Docs, Meet), YouTube, and publicly exposed Google Cloud APIs (Cloud Storage, BigQuery, Cloud SQL public IPs) without transiting public commercial internet service providers.
  • CRITICAL ROUTING BOUNDARY: Peering cannot route to private RFC 1918 VPC addresses. Google's edge peering routers strictly drop private RFC 1918 packets and reject all private route advertisements from peering partners. Furthermore, Google Peering is not an internet transit service and will never provide a default route (0.0.0.0/0) to the general internet.

Direct Peering vs. Carrier Peering

  • Direct Peering: Established directly between an enterprise Autonomous System (ASN) and Google ASN 15169 at a shared Internet Exchange Point (IXP) facility.
    • Prerequisites: The enterprise must possess a publicly registered Autonomous System Number (ASN), publicly routable IPv4/IPv6 address blocks, an entry in PeeringDB, a 24x7 Network Operations Center (NOC), and must establish dual physical cross-connects of at least 10 Gbps.
    • SLA: Zero SLA. Google does not offer an availability SLA on Direct Peering sessions.
  • Carrier Peering: For enterprise organizations that consume substantial Google Workspace or public API bandwidth but do not maintain a registered public ASN or physical presence at an IXP. The organization connects to a certified telecommunications carrier partner (such as AT&T, Verizon, or Colt), and the partner handles public BGP peering with Google ASN 15169 on the customer's behalf.

Single-Interface Classic VPN Outage During Maintenance · field case

Situation and impact: Brightloaf's regional delivery dispatch platform relied on a legacy Classic VPN tunnel to connect its central production distribution center to a primary logistics warehouse router in Chicago. During scheduled Google Cloud infrastructure hypervisor maintenance, the single public IP gateway host was evacuated and rebooted. The IPsec tunnel collapsed completely for 18 minutes. Dispatchers could not transmit picking and routing manifests to warehouse floor tablets, halting 80 refrigerated bakery delivery trucks, idling 160 drivers, and risking $1.8M in perishable goods spoilage.

Constraints: Must guarantee 99.99% tunnel availability; failover between redundant tunnels must execute in under 2 seconds without terminating active transport TCP sockets; zero manual routing table edits permitted during failover events.

Diagnostic sequence and root cause: 1. Queried the Classic VPN tunnel operational state: 

gcloud compute vpn-tunnels describe classic-tunnel-chicago   --region=us-central1   --format="yaml(status,detailedStatus)"
Output revealed status: FIRST_HANDSHAKE and detailedStatus: Peer gateway is not responding. 2. Inspected Cloud Logging audit trails: 
gcloud logging read 'protoPayload.serviceName="compute.googleapis.com" AND protoPayload.methodName="compute.instances.hostMaintenance"'   --limit=5
Logs confirmed the underlying Google hypervisor underwent automated live migration and maintenance. Because Classic VPN provisions only a single public IP gateway on a single hardware chassis, no alternate hardware interface existed to maintain the IPsec security association.

Defensible remediation: 1. Decommissioned Classic VPN and provisioned a Cloud HA VPN gateway, generating two distinct public IPv4 addresses (Interface 0: 35.240.10.14 and Interface 1: 34.102.44.82) anchored to independent Google cluster failure domains: 

gcloud compute vpn-gateways create ha-vpn-chicago   --network=prod-shared-vpc   --region=us-central1
2. Created a Cloud Router with dynamic eBGP (ASN 65001) and established dual IPsec tunnels to the warehouse edge router: 
gcloud compute routers create router-ha-chicago   --network=prod-shared-vpc   --region=us-central1   --asn=65001
3. Configured dynamic BGP peering sessions over both tunnel interfaces, enabling active/active ECMP route advertisement with 20-second hold timers.

Verification: Simulated a host maintenance event by administrative shutdown of Interface 0's BGP peering. Monitored Cloud Router metrics: BGP session transitioned to DOWN on Interface 0 in 1.2 seconds, and Interface 1 absorbed 100% of outbound packets with 0 dropped TCP segments and sub-1ms failover latency.

Alternative and residual risk: Ensure the on-premises peer gateway supports independent BGP peering sessions over dual IPsec tunnels; if the peer gateway only possesses a single public IP, configure two tunnels from that single IP terminating on GCP Interface 0 and Interface 1 respectively.

Partner Interconnect Svc Provider Central Node Blackout · field case

Situation and impact: Brightloaf's enterprise ERP core accounting database replicated journal transactions between its Chicago corporate headquarters and Google Cloud us-central1 over a Partner Interconnect deployment. The company configured two VLAN attachments to achieve redundancy, but procured both attachments through a single regional service provider. During quarter-end financial reconciliation, a catastrophic supervisor module failure on the partner's central aggregation switch crashed both VLAN circuits simultaneously. Private connectivity went dark for 9 hours. Inability to reconcile ledger transactions forced an emergency halt to financial reporting and triggered contractual non-compliance penalties totaling $420,000.

Constraints: Must eliminate single-provider dependency; must achieve wire-speed 100 Gbps private throughput; all data in transit across physical lines must meet regulatory encryption standards (MACsec).

Diagnostic sequence and root cause: 1. Inspected VLAN attachment status on Cloud Router: 

gcloud compute interconnects attachments describe vlan-partner-chicago-01   --region=us-central1   --format="yaml(state,adminEnabled,googleReferenceId)"
Both attachments displayed state: OS_UNAVAILABLE. 2. Traceroute analysis revealed that both virtual VLAN circuits shared the exact same service provider Multi-Protocol Label Switching (MPLS) provider edge (PE) router chassis in Chicago. While the customer had two virtual attachments, both attachments shared a single physical failure domain at the provider's central switching fabric.

Defensible remediation: 1. Re-architected hybrid transit to Dedicated Interconnect terminating directly in Equinix Chicago (CH1): 

gcloud compute interconnects create dedicated-chicago-01   --customer-name="Brightloaf Enterprise"   --interconnect-type=DEDICATED   --link-type=LINK_TYPE_ETHERNET_100G_LR   --location=equinix-ch1   --requested-link-count=1

gcloud compute interconnects create dedicated-chicago-02   --customer-name="Brightloaf Enterprise"   --interconnect-type=DEDICATED   --link-type=LINK_TYPE_ETHERNET_100G_LR   --location=equinix-ch1   --requested-link-count=1
2. Assigned Circuit 1 to Edge Availability Domain 1 (EAD-1) and Circuit 2 to Edge Availability Domain 2 (EAD-2), ensuring termination on physically separated Google routing hardware. 3. Enabled hardware-based line-rate MACsec (IEEE 802.1AE) Layer 2 encryption using Cloud KMS-managed pre-shared keys.

Verification: Executed an optical power loopback test and verified MACsec session establishment. Artificially disabled the EAD-1 cross-connect interface on the customer router; Cloud Router detected BGP session loss and transferred 100% of ERP replication traffic to the EAD-2 attachment in 1.4 seconds with zero transaction drops.

Alternative and residual risk: If Dedicated Interconnect lead time (typically 3 to 8 weeks) is prohibitive, maintain short-term connectivity by ordering Partner Interconnect VLAN attachments through two completely distinct telecommunications carriers operating in separate facilities.

Multi-Cloud Latency Degrading Real-Time Order Settlement · field case

Situation and impact: Brightloaf operated a hybrid multi-cloud e-commerce architecture: the customer catalog and shopping cart frontend ran in AWS us-east-1 (backed by AWS Aurora), while the real-time fraud scoring, credit authorization, and analytical settlement engine ran in Google Cloud us-east4 (backed by BigQuery and Vertex AI). Multi-cloud traffic traversed a site-to-site IPsec tunnel across the public internet. During a Black Friday flash sale, public transit congestion caused packet latency to spike from 18ms to 140ms, with packet loss exceeding 3.2%. High latency triggered transaction timeout aborts across 2,400 checkout sessions, producing $650,000 in abandoned orders.

Constraints: Round-trip latency between AWS and GCP must remain strictly under 5 milliseconds; must support MTU 1500 without IPsec packet fragmentation; zero exposure to public internet routing or intermediate transit providers.

Diagnostic sequence and root cause: 1. Executed a bidirectional network path trace between AWS and GCP instances: 

traceroute -T -p 443 10.10.20.15
Trace output showed 14 intermediate Autonomous System (AS) hops across commercial transit ISPs with severe packet jitter. 2. Inspected MTU and IPsec fragmentation metrics: 
ping -M do -s 1472 10.10.20.15
Packets exceeding 1420 bytes were fragmented due to IPsec encapsulation overhead, triggering severe TCP retransmissions and window collapse.

Defensible remediation: 1. Ordered Cross-Cloud Interconnect between Google Cloud us-east4 and AWS Direct Connect in Ashburn, VA: 

gcloud compute interconnects create cross-cloud-aws-east4   --interconnect-type=DEDICATED   --link-type=LINK_TYPE_ETHERNET_100G_LR   --location=equinix-dc2   --remote-location=aws-ashburn-1   --requested-link-count=1
2. Created an InterconnectAttachment configured with standard MTU 1500: 
gcloud compute interconnects attachments dedicated create vlan-cross-cloud-aws   --interconnect=cross-cloud-aws-east4   --region=us-east4   --router=router-aws-transit   --mtu=1500
3. Configured dynamic BGP peering with the AWS Direct Connect Gateway utilizing BGP MD5 authentication.

Verification: Initiated continuous 100,000 ICMP echo probes with 1500-byte payloads across the Cross-Cloud Interconnect link. Measured round-trip latency stabilized at a deterministic 2.4 milliseconds with 0.00% packet loss. Zero transaction timeouts were observed during synthetic load testing simulating 12,000 concurrent checkout validations.

Alternative and residual risk: For disaster recovery multi-cloud resilience, deploy a secondary Cross-Cloud Interconnect port in a separate facility (e.g., AWS us-west-2 to GCP us-west1) configured with higher BGP MED metrics.

BGP Spoke Sprawl Resolved via Network Connectivity Center · field case

Situation and impact: Brightloaf expanded rapidly by acquiring 45 regional retail bakeries. Each bakery operated a Fortinet SD-WAN appliance with point-to-point IPsec VPN connections configured to Cloud Routers across 4 production VPCs. Due to manual, uncoordinated BGP peering configurations, a network technician at a newly acquired bakery accidentally configured their edge router to advertise a default route (0.0.0.0/0) into GCP. The rogue default route propagated across Cloud Routers, hijacking all outbound internet-bound API calls, cloud storage uploads, and payment gateway traffic. Cloud egress was blackholed for 14 enterprise branches for over 3 hours.

Constraints: Must eliminate unmanaged point-to-point mesh routing; must enforce strict centralized route filtering to prevent rogue default route propagation; branch-to-branch traffic must traverse Google's backbone without hairpinned on-premises transit.

Diagnostic sequence and root cause: 1. Dumped the active VPC route table: 

gcloud compute routes list   --filter="network:prod-shared-vpc AND destRange:0.0.0.0/0"   --format="table(name,destRange,nextHopIp,priority)"
Output showed an unexpected dynamic BGP route with priority 100 pointing to the branch SD-WAN appliance IP. 2. Verified that Cloud Router's dynamic routing mode had accepted the unsummarized, unfiltered 0.0.0.0/0 prefix from the branch BGP peer and immediately installed it into the VPC routing fabric, overriding the default internet gateway route.

Defensible remediation: 1. Created a centralized Network Connectivity Center (NCC) Hub: 

gcloud network-connectivity hubs create enterprise-transit-hub   --description="Global Enterprise Transit Control Plane"
2. Attached the 4 VPC networks as NCC VPC Spokes. 3. Deployed redundant Router Appliance VM instances in a dedicated transit VPC and attached them as NCC Router Appliance Spokes: 
gcloud network-connectivity spokes linked-router-appliances create branch-router-appliance-spoke   --hub=enterprise-transit-hub   --region=us-central1   --router-appliance-instance=projects/brightloaf-net/zones/us-central1-a/instances/sdwan-gw-01,ip=10.100.0.15   --router-appliance-instance=projects/brightloaf-net/zones/us-central1-b/instances/sdwan-gw-02,ip=10.100.0.16
4. Implemented strict BGP route advertisement filters on Cloud Router, restricting accepted incoming prefixes strictly to approved enterprise branch subnets in 10.0.0.0/8 and explicitly rejecting 0.0.0.0/0.

Verification: Injected a test 0.0.0.0/0 route announcement from a staging branch router. Confirmed via Cloud Router BGP status that the route was explicitly suppressed by route policy filters and never installed into the VPC routing table.

Alternative and residual risk: Ensure Router Appliance VM instances have IP forwarding enabled at the Compute Engine NIC level; deploy Router Appliance instances across multiple availability zones to eliminate single VM failure risks.

False 99.99% Architecture Rejected by Enterprise Audit · field case

Situation and impact: A healthcare telemedicine platform hosting electronic health record (EHR) workloads claimed a 99.99% availability SLA for its hybrid connectivity in marketing and customer service contracts. During an annual regulatory SOC2 Type II and HIPAA infrastructure audit, external auditors discovered that all four 10G Dedicated Interconnect circuits were terminated within the same physical colocation building (Equinix Ashburn DC1). Two weeks later, a municipal water main burst flooded the underground conduit vault of the Ashburn facility, shorting out all fiber entrance tubes. The entire hybrid fabric collapsed simultaneously, causing an 11-hour total outage, halting hospital patient telemetry, and triggering a 25% insurance policy liability penalty.

Constraints: Must formally qualify for Google Cloud's contractual 99.99% SLA; must survive complete physical destruction of an entire metropolitan colocation building; remaining circuits must absorb 100% of peak database replication load without packet drop.

Diagnostic sequence and root cause: 1. Inspected physical circuit metadata across the deployment: 

gcloud compute interconnects list   --format="table(name,location,linkType,operationalStatus)"
Output confirmed that all 4 interconnects were provisioned in equinix-dc1 (Ashburn, VA). 2. While two circuits connected to EAD-1 and two connected to EAD-2, all circuits shared the same geographic metropolitan area, building structural foundation, and municipal civil utilities. Google Cloud documentation explicitly mandates that a 99.99% SLA requires deployment across two distinct metropolitan areas and two distinct GCP regions.

Defensible remediation: 1. Re-architected hybrid connectivity across dual metros: - Metro A (Ashburn): Two circuits terminating on EAD-1 and EAD-2, connected to Cloud Router 1 in us-east4. - Metro B (Chicago): Two circuits terminating on EAD-1 and EAD-2, connected to Cloud Router 2 in us-central1. 2. Enabled GLOBAL Dynamic Routing on the production VPC: 

gcloud compute networks update prod-shared-vpc   --bgp-routing-mode=GLOBAL
3. Configured deterministic BGP MED metrics: 
gcloud compute routers update-bgp-peer router-ashburn   --peer-name=bgp-peer-ashburn-01   --region=us-east4   --advertised-route-priority=100

gcloud compute routers update-bgp-peer router-chicago   --peer-name=bgp-peer-chicago-01   --region=us-central1   --advertised-route-priority=200

Verification: Simulated a total metropolitan blackout by administratively shutting down all BGP sessions on the Ashburn Cloud Router. Within 2.8 seconds, Global Dynamic Routing shifted all inbound and outbound enterprise traffic to the Chicago Cloud Router in us-central1 with zero TCP session resets.

Alternative and residual risk: Ensure on-premises backhaul fiber between Ashburn and Chicago possesses independent physical right-of-way routing; verify that surviving circuits in Chicago have sufficient contracted bandwidth to prevent throttling during failover.

Attempting Private VPC Transit Over Direct Peering · field case

Situation and impact: Brightloaf's data engineering team began migrating 120 TB of on-premises customer databases to Google Cloud SQL and BigQuery. Seeking to avoid the multi-week lead time and port costs of Dedicated Interconnect, the network engineering team configured a 10G Direct Peering session with Google ASN 15169 at the Equinix Ashburn Internet Exchange Point (IXP). Once BGP peering was established, the team initiated test database replication from on-premises host 172.16.50.10 to a private VPC Cloud SQL instance at 10.10.100.5. 100% of packets were dropped immediately. The failed cutover stalled the database migration for six weeks, incurring $180,000 in delayed-milestone consultant fees.

Constraints: Must enable secure, high-bandwidth private routing to RFC 1918 VPC addresses; must reserve peering links strictly for public SaaS services; must maintain strict compliance separation between internal database traffic and public internet traffic.

Diagnostic sequence and root cause: 1. Inspected BGP route advertisements on the on-premises border router: 

show ip bgp neighbors 198.51.100.1 advertised-routes
Output showed the router was attempting to announce RFC 1918 prefix 172.16.0.0/16 to Google ASN 15169. 2. Inspected routes received from Google ASN 15169: 
show ip bgp neighbors 198.51.100.1 routes
Output revealed only public Google IP prefixes (e.g., 142.250.0.0/15, 172.217.0.0/16). Google edge peering routers automatically drop private RFC 1918 prefix announcements and strictly refuse to route private traffic into customer VPCs.

Defensible remediation: 1. Enforced strict architectural segregation: - Reserved the Direct Peering connection exclusively for Google Workspace (Gmail, Drive) and public Google APIs (Cloud Storage). - Provisioned Partner Interconnect with redundant VLAN attachments for private RFC 1918 VPC database replication: 

gcloud compute interconnects attachments partner create vlan-db-migration-01   --region=us-east4   --router=router-db-migration   --edge-availability-domain=AVAILABILITY_DOMAIN_1
2. Configured on-premises policy-based routing to ensure all 10.0.0.0/8 and 172.16.0.0/12 traffic routes exclusively across the Cloud Interconnect VLAN attachments.

Verification: Executed an IP route test from the on-premises database server to the private Cloud SQL IP (10.10.100.5). Traffic successfully forwarded across the Interconnect VLAN attachment with sub-2ms latency. Concurrently verified that Google Workspace traffic (Drive, Gmail) continued to route efficiently over Direct Peering.

Alternative and residual risk: If direct peering physical requirements (such as 10G minimum circuits or PeeringDB registration) cannot be sustained, migrate public SaaS traffic to Carrier Peering through a certified telecommunications provider.

Exercise 1: Deploy a Cloud HA VPN Gateway with Dual BGP Tunnels

Goal: Provision a production Cloud HA VPN gateway with dual interfaces, configure dynamic eBGP routing via Cloud Router, and establish redundant IPsec tunnels qualifying for the 99.99% availability SLA.

Mode: production practice · Prerequisite: Day 55 exit artifacts.

  1. Create Cloud Router with Custom ASN: 
    gcloud compute routers create router-ha-east4   --network=prod-shared-vpc   --region=us-east4   --asn=65001
  2. Create HA VPN Gateway: 
    gcloud compute vpn-gateways create ha-vpn-east4   --network=prod-shared-vpc   --region=us-east4
  3. Inspect Gateway Public Interfaces: 
    gcloud compute vpn-gateways describe ha-vpn-east4   --region=us-east4   --format="table(name,vpnInterfaces[].id,vpnInterfaces[].ipAddress)"
    Verify that Google Cloud assigned two distinct public IPv4 addresses (interface 0 and interface 1) on separate Google hardware clusters.
  4. Create External Peer VPN Gateway Resource: 
    gcloud compute external-vpn-gateways create onprem-peer-gw   --interfaces 0=198.51.100.10,1=198.51.100.20
  5. Create Dual IPsec Tunnels: 
    gcloud compute vpn-tunnels create tunnel-ha-0   --region=us-east4   --vpn-gateway=ha-vpn-east4   --interface=0   --peer-external-gateway=onprem-peer-gw   --peer-external-gateway-interface=0   --shared-secret="ComplexPresharedKey987654"   --router=router-ha-east4

gcloud compute vpn-tunnels create tunnel-ha-1   --region=us-east4   --vpn-gateway=ha-vpn-east4   --interface=1   --peer-external-gateway=onprem-peer-gw   --peer-external-gateway-interface=1   --shared-secret="ComplexPresharedKey987654"   --router=router-ha-east4
  6. Add Cloud Router BGP Interfaces and Peers: 
    gcloud compute routers add-interface router-ha-east4   --region=us-east4   --interface-name=if-tunnel-0   --ip-address=169.254.10.1   --mask-length=30   --vpn-tunnel=tunnel-ha-0

gcloud compute routers add-bgp-peer router-ha-east4   --region=us-east4   --peer-name=bgp-peer-0   --interface=if-tunnel-0   --peer-ip-address=169.254.10.2   --peer-asn=65002

gcloud compute routers add-interface router-ha-east4   --region=us-east4   --interface-name=if-tunnel-1   --ip-address=169.254.10.5   --mask-length=30   --vpn-tunnel=tunnel-ha-1

gcloud compute routers add-bgp-peer router-ha-east4   --region=us-east4   --peer-name=bgp-peer-1   --interface=if-tunnel-1   --peer-ip-address=169.254.10.6   --peer-asn=65002
  7. Verify BGP Session States: 
    gcloud compute routers get-status router-ha-east4   --region=us-east4   --format="yaml(result.bgpPeerStatus)"
Expected result / acceptance

Both VPN tunnels show status ESTABLISHED. Cloud Router displays active BGP sessions over both interfaces, qualifying for the 99.99% availability SLA.

Troubleshooting

If BGP status is CONNECTING, verify that on-premises firewalls permit UDP ports 500 and 4500 (IKE and IPsec NAT-T) and IP protocol 50 (ESP).

Cleanup and cost

Delete tunnels, external VPN gateways, and HA VPN gateways after testing to avoid hourly gateway charges.
