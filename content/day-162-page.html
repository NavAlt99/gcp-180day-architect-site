1. Read the current standard-exam guide and distinguish renewal scope: technical mechanics

The current standard guide organizes the exam around six domains: designing and planning a cloud solution architecture; managing and provisioning infrastructure; designing for security and compliance; analyzing and optimizing technical and business processes; managing implementation; and ensuring solution and operations excellence. The guide gives approximate weights, but the practical task is to connect each domain to evidence and decision rules rather than memorize percentages.

Exam-domain evidence map
DomainEvidence to mapRepair signal
Design and planRequirements, ADRs, migrations, diagrams and cost trade-offs.Can name products but cannot explain why.
Manage/provisionIAM, networking, storage, compute, orchestration and automation artifacts.Cannot identify owner or provisioning boundary.

Operations excellenceSLOs, observability, recovery, release and reliability experiments.Uses a dashboard or topology as proof of resilience.

Renewal has a different scope and format. Read the current certification page and guide before deciding which path applies; do not infer eligibility from a practice score.

Decision dossier: exam-domain mapping

Make Read the current standard-exam guide and distinguish renewal scope reviewable rather than merely plausible. Its material boundaries are requirement interpretation, option elimination, evidence-backed trade-offs, and uncertainty handling. Begin with a fact register: quote or link the supplied statement, record its owner, label it supplied, observed, inferred, or unknown, and name the artifact that could disprove the interpretation. Reasoning sequence. Write an invariant in plain language—such as “one source version has one publishable derived version,” “a restricted actor cannot retrieve a protected record,” or “rollback restores an internally consistent state.” Then map the invariant to a signal, owner, response, and residual risk. Keep selected and rejected options side by side.

Changed-constraint rehearsal. Change one material condition: lost connectivity, shorter cutover, unavailable dependency, stricter retention, larger corpus, delayed reviewer, or a different recovery target. Identify the first failed boundary, safe degraded behavior, stop condition, and evidence to regenerate. Tabletop evidence predicts behavior only; it cannot prove production, compliance, cost, or exam claims.

Architectural evaluation questions

  • Which requirement bounds the selected option?
  • What observable evidence supports the decision?
  • Which changed constraint would reverse the choice?
  • Who owns the failure response and review?

2. Record why sample-question answers fail the stated requirements: technical mechanics

Question analysis is not just answer memorization. Parse the actor, workload, state, constraint, desired outcome, operational overhead and “must not” clause. Eliminate options that violate a hard requirement before comparing soft trade-offs. When two options remain, prefer the one that meets the requirement with less unnecessary operational burden and fewer unverified assumptions.

Error taxonomy

Tag each miss as knowledge gap, requirements misread, scope/wording trap, calculation, time pressure or unsupported assumption. Each tag gets a different repair.

Exercise 1: Build the six-domain evidence map

Goal: Map existing artifacts to the current standard guide and identify the weakest domain.

Expected result: A source-dated six-domain map with evidence links, confidence labels and a prioritized repair plan.

Mode: local/tabletop with synthetic data; no cloud access or spend · Prerequisite: Bring the prior case or exam artifact and record it unavailable if missing.

Preflight: Use `day-162-01-build-the-six-domain-evidence-map.md`; record the source, access date, prior artifact, owner and synthetic-data boundary.

Exact execution

  1. Stage 1 — Preflight and validate assumptions/environment. Create day-162-01-build-the-six-domain-evidence-map.md. Record Day 162, the prior artifact, the source https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/professional_cloud_architect_exam_guide_english.pdf, the access date, a named owner and the statement that the exercise uses synthetic or tabletop evidence only.
  2. Stage 2 — Prepare the target, inputs, or backing resources. Confirm scope. Open the official certification page and standard guide; record standard versus renewal and the date checked.
  3. Stage 3 — Author the plan, configuration, or analysis. Map evidence. For each domain list at least two existing artifacts and one decision rule you can defend.
  4. Stage 4 — Execute or simulate the planned change. Score confidence. Use New, Familiar or Demonstrated; cite the artifact rather than self-rating alone.
  5. Stage 5 — Inspect expected state and verify outcomes. Find the gap. Choose the weakest domain by missing evidence or inability to explain a trade-off.
  6. Stage 6 — Rehearse a bounded failure, edge case, or decision challenge. Plan repair. Write a small corrective exercise and a source link; do not add unrelated scope.
  7. Stage 7 — Diagnose evidence and record remediation/decision. Save. Export the dated domain map and remediation list.
  8. Stage 8 — Clean up or close out the exercise. Save day-162-01-build-the-six-domain-evidence-map.md, link it from the daily artifact, keep the evidence, remove only disposable copies and state what needs an authorized or production verification.
