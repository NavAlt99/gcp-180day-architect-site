Day 35 — Gate 2: Sandbox and identity readiness

Outcome: Review and remediate Days 18–34. Demonstrate active identity/project, narrow allowed and denied access, a complete operation lifecycle, and confirmed inventory cleanup — without the original walkthroughs.

Entry prerequisites:

Day 26 (credentials and tokens), Day 29 (cost exports, quota and capacity), Day 34 (first controlled cloud resource operation); bring their exit artifacts.

Exit artifact

A scored G2 checklist, budget/identity evidence, and resolved critical access or cleanup gaps. Label every result as live (cloud access confirmed) or supplied trace (tabletop). Critical failures — inability to deny unauthorized access, failure to recover inventory, or unresolved orphaned resources — must be remediated before proceeding to Day 36.

Gate rule

Gate 2 reviews and remediates prior material only. Do not introduce new GCP services, IAM roles, or architectural concepts on this checkpoint day. Every practice step in this page points back to an earlier day's established material.

1 · Topics of the day

Prerequisite review and remediation (Days 18–34)

Gate 2 covers the Cloud environment and identity block: Days 18–34. Its scope is the set of skills needed to work safely in a cloud sandbox — setting up cost controls, understanding the resource hierarchy, managing credentials, controlling access through IAM, understanding billing and quota limits, making region and network tier decisions, and executing a basic VM lifecycle with error classification.

Remediation means returning to the specific earlier day whose exit artifact is missing, incomplete, or contains a critical failure. A critical failure is one that would allow unauthorized access to remain, leave a chargeable resource undeleted, or misclassify an error in a way that could cause production harm. Minor gaps (e.g., a tabletop result where a live trace was preferred) can be logged and carried forward.

Problem preview: A learner marks the G2 checklist complete but has not confirmed a denied access attempt — only successful calls were tested. Business impact: the assumed narrow identity boundary may be wider than believed; subsequent labs may grant permissions assuming the baseline is already restricted, compounding the over-permission risk from Day 34's propagation scenario.

Gate 2 scored criteria and self-assessment

The five-dimension rubric scores each dimension 0–3: 0 = absent or incorrect, 1 = partial, 2 = adequate with stated limits, 3 = clear and reproducible. Gates 2–6 require at least 2 in every dimension and 12/15 overall. The five dimensions are: correctness, requirement traceability, evidence quality, failure/recovery reasoning, and communication.

Gate 2 specifically requires: proof of active identity and project, a narrow allowed access demonstration, a deliberate denied access demonstration, a complete operation lifecycle trace (create → observe → inventory → delete), and confirmed empty inventory after cleanup.

Problem preview: A learner scores 14/15 overall but rates failure/recovery reasoning as 2, having demonstrated cleanup but not documented why they chose a label-based filter over a name-based delete command. The gap is not critical but should be noted: if the name-based command were used in a real lab, an orphaned VM from an earlier crash would escape cleanup.

Gate 2 scored criteria and self-assessment

G2 gate proof requirements

Gate 2 requires four concrete demonstrations in the exit artifact, in addition to the scored checklist:

  1. Active identity proof. Output of gcloud auth list showing the active account; output of gcloud config get project showing the correct project ID. If impersonating a service account, show the impersonation token, not just the SA email.
  2. Narrow allowed access. A command that succeeds using the narrowly scoped principal — e.g., listing VMs in the sandbox project as a Compute Instance Viewer — recorded with the exact principal and response.
  3. Deliberate denied access. A command that fails with PERMISSION_DENIED using the same or a different principal attempting a broader action (e.g., attempting to delete a VM as a Compute Viewer). Record the error response. This step is critical: without it, the assumed boundary may not exist.
  4. Complete operation lifecycle. The Day 34 exit artifact, or a repeated demonstration: create → operation describe → instance describe (RUNNING) → label inventory → delete → empty inventory.

3 · Real-world problems and solutions

The scenarios below are illustrative examples grounded in the Day 35 Gate 2 context. They are not observed production incidents. All results are tabletop predictions unless labeled otherwise.

Topic 1 field case — Gate checklist completed without a demonstrated denial

Situation and business impact

A Brightloaf platform engineer completes the G2 self-assessment and scores 13/15 overall, with a 2 in every dimension. However, the "Deliberate denied access" requirement was satisfied by noting "PERMISSION_DENIED is documented in the Day 34 incident diagram" rather than by running an actual denied command during this Gate 2 session. The gate checklist is marked complete and the engineer proceeds to Day 36.

During Day 36 the engineer creates a VM with a wider-than-intended service account scope, assuming the narrow boundary established in Day 26 is still in place. Because the Day 35 denial demonstration was not re-run, there is no confirmation that the boundary survived changes made between Days 26 and 34 (including the accidental escalation described in the Day 34 Problem 1 scenario). Business impact: a VM runs with a service account that can read and potentially modify resources outside the intended lab boundary.

Constraints

  • The Gate 2 requirement is explicit: the denial must be demonstrated in this session, not inferred from an earlier diagram.
  • The engineer must not create new resources to satisfy the denial test — an existing resource and an existing narrow principal are sufficient.
  • No new IAM roles should be granted to satisfy the denial; use the existing principal with its existing scope.

Root cause

A reference to a prior diagram was accepted as equivalent to a demonstrated command. Gate 2's failure/recovery reasoning dimension explicitly requires the denial to be "attempted and error recorded." The gate rubric distinguishes a design-exercise result from a live or supplied-trace command output.

Diagnostic sequence

  1. Return to the G2 checklist and locate the "Deliberate denied access" row.
  2. Identify the narrowest principal in the sandbox project (e.g., a service account with only Compute Viewer).
  3. Attempt an action that principal cannot perform (e.g., delete a VM, create a firewall rule).
  4. Record the full error response — command, principal, resource, error code — in the G2 exit artifact.
  5. Re-score the failure/recovery reasoning dimension and update the total.

Defensible solution

Run the denial command before filing the G2 checklist. Treat a diagram reference as evidence of understanding, not as evidence of behavior. If cloud access is unavailable, document the denial as a supplied trace — state what command would be run, what error is expected, and label the result explicitly as "tabletop prediction, not live."

Verification

After running the denial: the G2 artifact must contain the exact command, active principal, resource attempted, and the PERMISSION_DENIED (or equivalent) response. A reviewer should be able to reproduce the denial from the note alone without guessing at the principal or resource.

Residual risk

In a sandbox where all resources are in one project and the learner is the project owner, it may be difficult to demonstrate a genuine denial without impersonating a service account. Impersonation requires the Service Account Token Creator role; if that was not set up in Day 26, the denial test must be documented as a tabletop prediction and labeled accordingly.

Topic 2 field case — Orphaned resource discovered at Gate 2 time

Situation and business impact

During the Gate 2 inventory check, the learner runs a label-based list command and discovers a VM with label env=lab that was created during Day 29's quota-check exercise and was never deleted. The VM has been running for multiple days. The sandbox budget alert threshold (Day 18) was set but the alert email was sent to an unmonitored inbox. The learner's exit artifact for Day 29 noted "created for quota inspection; cleanup pending" but the cleanup was never completed.

Business impact: unexpected compute cost accruing against the sandbox budget; a resource in an unknown state (the VM may have been modified between Day 29 and Day 35); the Gate 2 inventory/cleanup requirement cannot be satisfied until this resource is resolved.

Constraints

  • The Gate 2 session must not create new resources to fix the orphan — delete only.
  • The deletion must be confirmed with an empty label-based inventory before the G2 artifact is filed.
  • The budget alert inbox must be updated before filing; this is a critical gap.

Root cause

The Day 29 exit artifact contained a "cleanup pending" note that was never acted on. Gate 2 specifically requires "confirmed empty inventory" — this is the safety net that catches exactly this class of gap. The root cause is a disconnect between a noted gap and a tracked remediation action.

Diagnostic sequence

  1. Run the full-project inventory, not just a label filter, to discover any other orphaned resources: gcloud compute instances list --project=PROJECT_ID --format="table(name,zone,status,labels)".
  2. For each orphan, determine its origin day from its label, describe it to confirm no dependent resources (attached disks, static IPs), and delete it.
  3. After each deletion, run the label inventory to confirm the count decremented.
  4. Update the budget alert inbox to an actively monitored email before filing the G2 artifact.

Defensible solution

Delete the orphaned VM and confirm with an empty label-based list. Update the budget alert recipient. Add a note to the G2 artifact: "Orphaned VM from Day 29 found and deleted on [date]; budget alert recipient updated." Going forward, the exit artifact for every day that creates a cloud resource must include a deletion confirmation before the artifact is filed.

Verification

After cleanup: gcloud compute instances list --project=PROJECT_ID returns zero rows (or only intentionally retained resources with documented justification). The budget alert configuration shows an actively monitored recipient. Both are recorded in the G2 artifact.

Residual risk

A full-project list covers VMs but not other resource types (disks, static IPs, load balancer rules). A complete cleanup should also check: gcloud compute disks list --project=PROJECT_ID and gcloud compute addresses list --project=PROJECT_ID. If those were not listed in earlier labs, note the assumption as a minor gap in the G2 artifact.

4 · Step-by-step labs for each topic

Exercise 1 — Prerequisite review and gap identification

Goal: Review Days 18–34 exit artifacts, identify critical gaps and minor gaps, and produce a gap log that maps each gap to the day that must be re-checked.

Mode: tabletop review · Prerequisites: exit artifacts from Days 18–34 (complete or partial).

Preflight

Locate all exit artifact files from Days 18–34. If any day's artifact is missing entirely, mark it as a critical gap immediately — a missing artifact cannot be inferred from memory.

  1. Create the gap log. Open a new note: day-035-gap-log.md. Add a header row with columns: Day | Artifact present? | Critical gap? | Minor gap? | Remediation action | Done.
  2. Walk through each day. For Days 18–34, open the exit artifact (or note its absence). Consult the competency table in Part 2 (Topic 1) and mark each row. Specific items to check for each day are listed there. A day with no exit artifact is always a critical gap.
  3. Identify critical gaps. For each critical gap, write in the Remediation action column: the exact exercise number to repeat (from the linked day's Part 4), the specific command or output that was missing, and the file where the repaired evidence will be saved.
  4. Prioritize remediation. Order critical gaps from most recent to oldest (Day 34 → Day 18). Address the most recent gap first — it is most likely to have cascade effects on subsequent days.
  5. Verify and save. For each remediated gap, mark the Done column with the repair date and the new artifact file path. The gap log becomes part of the G2 exit artifact.
Expected result / acceptance

A completed gap log with every day from 18 to 34 assessed, all critical gaps identified and either remediated or explicitly deferred with a justification, and all minor gaps noted. The log must distinguish between a live result and a tabletop prediction for each entry.

Troubleshooting

If an artifact exists but is incomplete (e.g., has creation evidence but no deletion trace), treat it as a critical gap for the specific missing component only — do not repeat the entire day's lab. If an artifact is labeled "tabletop prediction" where a live result was required, note it as a minor gap and re-run the specific command if cloud access is now available.

Cleanup and cost

This exercise creates no cloud resources. If any earlier day's cleanup is identified as incomplete during this review, the deletion commands must be run as part of Exercise 2 before filing the G2 artifact.

Exercise 2 — G2 scored demonstration and checklist

Goal: Execute the four required Gate 2 demonstrations without the original walkthroughs, score the five-dimension rubric, file the complete G2 exit artifact.

Mode: cloud sandbox (preferred) or supplied trace (tabletop) · Prerequisites: Day 35 Exercise 1 gap log; access confirmed in Exercise 1; all critical gaps remediated or explicitly deferred.

Preflight

Open a fresh terminal. Do not re-read the earlier day pages — this is an unaided demonstration. Bring only your exit artifacts. If cloud access is unavailable, label every result as "supplied trace — tabletop prediction" and describe what the command would produce.

  1. Demonstration 1 — Active identity. Without prompting from this page, show the active account and active project. Record the command and the full output. Confirm the active role matches Day 26's evidence.
  2. Demonstration 2 — Narrow allowed access. Run one command that the narrow principal can execute successfully (e.g., list VMs, describe a disk). Record: command, active principal, resource, and the successful response. Note what the result proves (the role is effective) and what it does not prove (it does not prove the boundary is narrow — that requires the denial).
  3. Demonstration 3 — Deliberate denied access. Run one command that the narrow principal cannot execute (e.g., delete a VM as a Compute Viewer, create a firewall rule). Record: command, active principal, resource attempted, and the error response verbatim. This step is not satisfied by a diagram reference or a prior artifact — it must be a new command run in this session. If tabletop: describe the expected error in your own words and label it explicitly as "tabletop prediction."
  4. Demonstration 4 — VM operation lifecycle. Either reference the Day 34 exit artifact (if it contains all four phases: create → observe → inventory → delete → empty) or re-run the lifecycle now. If re-running: create one e2-micro VM, describe it, list it by label, delete it, and confirm an empty list. All four steps must be in the artifact.
  5. Full-project inventory check. Run gcloud compute instances list --project=PROJECT_ID --format="table(name,zone,status,labels)" and confirm the result matches expectations. If any orphaned VM appears, delete it now and record the deletion before proceeding.
  6. Score the rubric. For each of the five dimensions (Correctness, Requirement traceability, Evidence quality, Failure/recovery reasoning, Communication), assign a score of 0, 1, 2, or 3 using the rubric table in Part 2. Record the score and the specific evidence that supports it. Sum the scores. If any dimension is below 2 or the total is below 12, do not mark the gate as passed — return to the gap and repair it.
  7. Assemble the G2 artifact. Combine into day-035-g2-artifact.md: gap log (from Exercise 1), all four demonstration outputs, full-project inventory result, rubric scores with justification, total score, and a clear pass or remediate decision. Label every result as live or tabletop prediction.
Expected result / acceptance

A G2 artifact containing: active identity output, allowed access output, denied access output (or labeled tabletop), operation lifecycle trace, empty inventory confirmation, rubric scores (all ≥ 2, total ≥ 12/15), and a dated pass or remediate decision. If peer review was available, note reviewer name or role.

Troubleshooting

If the full-project inventory reveals an orphan: describe it, check for dependents, delete it, and re-run the list before scoring. If PERMISSION_DENIED appears where success was expected: re-check the active account with gcloud auth list and confirm the project; do not widen the role — diagnose the specific permission gap first. If a dimension scores below 2: identify the single missing piece of evidence and add it before re-scoring.

Cleanup and cost

If a VM was created in Demonstration 4, confirm it is deleted before filing the artifact. If any orphaned resource was found and deleted, confirm the budget alert inbox is updated and the deletion is recorded. No net new resources should remain after this gate session. Verify with a final empty-list command and include its output in the artifact.

Daily evidence

File day-035-g2-artifact.md containing: gap log from Exercise 1, all four Gate 2 demonstrations (active identity, allowed access, denied access, operation lifecycle), full-project empty-inventory confirmation, rubric scores for all five dimensions, total score, and a dated pass or remediate decision. Label every result as live or tabletop prediction. Remediate all critical gaps before proceeding to Day 36.
